## Required qualifiers

- Describe Harness as a proprietary public proof of concept, not a mature supported product.
- Describe public repositories and dated release material as inspectable evidence, not proof of adoption or universal correctness.
- Describe automated tests as evidence for tested paths. They do not establish complete correctness, complete security, adoption, or business impact.
- Describe confidential client work through implemented architecture and approved facts. Do not imply current deployment, acceptance, adoption, or business outcomes.
- Describe a form as configured or accepted by the site unless human inbox receipt has been verified.
- Describe local or offline behavior as the standard or core local workflow and disclose when external services change the boundary.
- Describe product and service availability as subject to written scope, compatibility, eligibility, and third-party terms where relevant.

## Forbidden public claims

- Guaranteed profit, returns, income, activity, win rate, fill, settlement, suitability, or uptime.
- Fixed AK47 public price, remaining-license count, artificial scarcity, or QA-check total unless expressly reauthorized with dated evidence and counsel review.
- Absolute AK47 refund or no-refund language outside counsel-approved signed terms.
- “Nothing leaves the machine,” “nothing leaves your disk,” or equivalent absolute privacy claims.
- “Parts lookup does not hallucinate” or another guarantee that an AI or retrieval system cannot be wrong.
- “Complete workflow,” “complete handoff,” “system complete,” or similar undefined completion claims.
- Client counts, users, downloads, revenue, rankings, conversion increases, cost savings, adoption, awards, or testimonials without direct evidence and publication authorization.
- Volatile test totals, CI-job totals, provider counts, version numbers, or repository status in evergreen marketing copy.
- Public menu prices, fixed schedules, included revision counts, support periods, payment percentages, fee credits, or ownership promises that are not confirmed current policy and mirrored in the controlling agreement.
- “Secure intake” without a defined, verified security claim.
- A promise that a human received, read, accepted, or replied to a form submission based only on website or provider status.

- cancellation, delivery, refund, and non-waivable legal rights;
- AK47 availability, eligibility, permitted use, risk acknowledgement, and license rights.

## AK47 counsel freeze

Do not materially expand or strengthen the public AK47 offer until counsel reviews the current page, signed-order template, license, refund and cancellation terms, event-contract disclosures, and Telegram disclaimer. Risk-reducing corrections remain allowed. Never describe the current copy as legally approved.

## Pre-publication check

Before publishing any new or revised claim:

1. Identify the exact evidence and its date.
2. Separate implementation, deployment, acceptance, adoption, and outcome.
3. Check whether client authorization is required.
4. Check whether the statement is controlled by a proposal, order, license, or provider term.
5. Remove volatile counts from evergreen copy or label them as a dated evidence snapshot.
6. Use `[NEED FACT]` when the evidence is missing or ambiguous.
7. Confirm market, privacy, security, and AI language is bounded rather than absolute.
8. Confirm forms do not request secrets or sensitive records.
9. For AK47, stop if the change expands the offer or legal position before counsel review.

## Instructions for AI-assisted rewrites

Preserve the meaning of approved claims. Do not infer outcomes from technical work. Do not convert tests into customers, publication into adoption, configuration into delivery, local-first into absolute privacy, or an implemented feature into a current production service. Do not restore a forbidden claim to improve conversion. If a requested fact is not supported here, output `[NEED FACT]` and ask for evidence.
